# Lesson 2 — Your Brain
**Goal: an AI that knows who they are, in about 15 minutes.**
**Time: 15 minutes. Hold that line.**

This is the lesson that makes Lesson 4 hit. Skip it and the inbox audit becomes a
generic summarizer — the thing everybody's already unimpressed by.

---

## What to say first

> "Right now this thing is brilliant and knows nothing about you. That's why everybody's
> AI sounds the same — they're all talking to a stranger.
>
> We're about to fix that in fifteen minutes. And here's the part that's going to feel
> strange: **it's going to interview you.** You don't write anything. You just answer,
> like we're having a conversation."

---

## Step 0 — Ask if they already have one

**Ask this before starting the interview.** Some people already built a brain — at a
workshop, in the Business Brain Builder, or on their own. Making them redo it is a
15-minute punishment for having done the work.

> "Quick check before we start — does anybody already have a brain file? A CLAUDE.md,
> or a doc where you've already laid out your business, your goals, how you talk?
>
> If you do, you're skipping this whole lesson. Two ways:
>
> **Drag it straight into this folder** and rename it `CLAUDE.md`. Done.
>
> Or **drag the file into the Claude window** and say *'turn this into my CLAUDE.md.'*
> It'll reformat whatever you've got — a Word doc, notes, an old profile — into the
> right shape.
>
> Either way you're done in thirty seconds. Go get a coffee, I'll catch up to you."

**If they bring an existing file, still do one thing:** open it and check for the four
sections the audit needs. Older brains often don't have them:

- How I make money right now
- **What a real opportunity looks like for me this quarter**
- Who matters
- What I want to stop spending time on

If any are missing, ask *only those questions* and append them. Two minutes, not fifteen.
The rest of their file stays exactly as it is — don't rewrite work they already did.

---

## Step 1 — Run the interview

*(For everyone who doesn't already have a brain.)*

Tell them to type this, in their own words:

> "Interview me and write my CLAUDE.md file."

Then the agent conducts the interview below. **Ask ONE question at a time.** Wait for the
answer. React to it like a person would before asking the next one.

---

## The interview — 9 questions, 4 sections

Keep it moving. If someone gives a one-word answer, ask one follow-up, then move on.
A short honest answer beats a long polished one.

### Who they are (2)
1. **"What do you actually do, and how long have you been doing it?"**
2. **"How do you make money right now — where does the bulk of it actually come from?"**
   → Push for specifics. "Referrals from past clients" beats "real estate."

### What they want (3)
3. **"What are you trying to make happen in the next 90 days? Be specific — a number,
   a deal, a change."**
4. **"When an opportunity shows up in your world, what does it actually look like?
   Who's the person, what are they asking for?"**
   → **This is the most important answer in the entire file.** It's what Lesson 4 uses
   to decide what "money" means. Don't accept "more clients." Get the real shape of it.
5. **"Who matters right now? Names — clients, partners, your broker, your lender."**

### What's draining them (2)
6. **"What part of the week do you dread?"**
7. **"What do you keep meaning to follow up on and never do?"**
   → They'll name it fast. Everybody knows. Write down exactly how they say it.

### How they sound (2)
8. **"How do you talk to a client you like? Formal, casual, straight-shooter?"**
9. **"Anything you'd never say? Words that don't sound like you?"**

---

## Step 2 — Write the file

Write `CLAUDE.md` in the project root, using **their words, not tidied-up versions.**
If they said "I hate cold calling and I'm terrible at follow-up," write that. The file
is useful in proportion to how honest it is.

Structure it exactly like `CLAUDE.md`'s template sections — especially these four,
because the `email-audit` skill reads them by name:

- **How I make money right now**
- **What a real opportunity looks like for me this quarter**
- **Who matters**
- **What I want to stop spending time on**

---

## Step 3 — Show them the file

Open it. Read a few lines back to them out loud.

> "That's your brain. Every single time you open this folder, it reads that first —
> so it never starts from zero again. You never have to explain yourself twice."

Then the part that sells the whole thing:

> "Everybody in this room is about to run the exact same command on their inbox in a few
> minutes. Everybody's going to get a different answer. **This file is why.**"

---

## Step 4 — Tell them it's alive

> "This isn't a form you fill out once. When something changes — new goal, new market,
> you drop a service — you tell it, and it updates the file. It grows with you."

---

## Done when

- [ ] `CLAUDE.md` exists and has all four required sections filled in —
      **whether it came from the interview or from a file they already had**
- [ ] The "what an opportunity looks like" answer is **specific**, not generic
- [ ] They've seen the file and recognize themselves in it

Mark Lesson 2 complete in `progress.md`.

---

## If someone is stuck

Some people freeze on question 4 — they've never had to define an opportunity out loud.
Give them the shape, not the answer:

> "Say a name. Who was the last person who could've been a deal and wasn't?
> Start there — what would they have been asking you for?"

**Don't let anyone spend more than 15 minutes here.** A good-enough brain that gets used
today beats a perfect one they never finish. They can deepen it later — that's what the
full Business Brain Builder is for.
