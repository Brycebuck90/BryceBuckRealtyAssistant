# Lesson 4 — The Sweep
**Goal: they see money they'd already lost.**
**Time: 10 minutes, then let it breathe.**

This is the payoff. Everything before it was setup. Don't rush it and don't talk over it.

---

## Step 1 — Set it up before you run it

> "In about thirty seconds you're going to see your last seven days sorted into three
> piles: money you're leaving on the table, stuff actually worth your time, and noise.
>
> Fair warning — the first pile is usually uncomfortable."

Say the warning. It turns the discomfort from a bad surprise into a shared moment.

---

## Step 2 — Run it

Have them type it in plain English, not as a command:

> "Audit my email."

> "Notice you didn't type a command. You asked. That's the whole point."

The `email-audit` skill runs: reads their `CLAUDE.md`, pulls 7 days, sorts three buckets,
drafts three replies.

---

## Step 3 — Sit in it

**Stop talking. Let them read.**

Then, only after they've had a minute:

> "Look at the top of that money list. How many days has that one been sitting?"

Ask a couple of people to say a number out loud. Someone in the room will have a
9-day-old message from a real person about a real deal. That number is the workshop.

> "Nobody in here is bad at their job. You're just buried. That message didn't get
> missed because you don't care — it got missed because four hundred other things
> came in on top of it.
>
> **That's the 80%.** And you just watched something else handle it in thirty seconds."

---

## Step 4 — The drafts

Open one. Read it out loud.

> "It wrote that as you, because it read your brain first. That's why it doesn't sound
> like a robot wrote it — it's got your voice, your situation, your priorities.
>
> And it's a **draft.** It's sitting in a folder waiting for you. Nothing was sent.
> Nothing gets sent without you. That's not a limitation — that's the philosophy.
> **AI drafts. You decide.** Your name goes on it, so you get the final word."

Then check their inbox with them:

> "Go look at your inbox right now. Same unread count. Nothing marked read, nothing moved,
> nothing gone. It read a week of your mail and left zero fingerprints.
>
> That's the default because I think it should be. But it's in `PERMISSIONS.md` and it's
> yours to change — if you'd rather it marked things as it goes, one line and it does."

Having them *verify* it with their own eyes beats being told. That's the moment the
guarantee stops being a claim.

---

## Step 5 — Name what they just built

> "Take stock of what happened in the last hour.
>
> You installed a tool. You taught it who you are. You gave it access, safely, and you
> understood exactly what you were giving. Then you gave it a job and it did the job.
>
> **That's an AI agent.** Not a chatbot you paste things into. A thing that knows you,
> has a job, and does it. You built one. Today.
>
> And here's what matters: **that inbox skill is one job.** The folder's the same.
> The brain's the same. Everything after this is just adding more jobs."

---

## Step 6 — Make it a habit before they leave

> "Monday morning, before you do anything else: open the folder, type 'audit my email.'
> Four minutes. You'll start your week knowing exactly who to call."

Have them actually put it on their calendar. In the room. Right now.
A skill they use once is a party trick. A skill they use every Monday changes their year.

---

## Done when

- [ ] The audit ran and returned real results from their real inbox
- [ ] They found at least one thing they'd genuinely missed
- [ ] Drafts exist in `drafts/`
- [ ] Monday is on their calendar

Mark Lesson 4 complete in `progress.md`.

---

## If someone's inbox comes back clean

It happens — a brand new agent, or someone genuinely on top of it.

**Do not manufacture urgency.** Say:

> "Yours is clean. That's a real answer, and it's worth knowing for sure instead of
> assuming. Run it in two weeks when things pile up — and that's the point: it's not
> a one-time trick, it's Monday morning, every Monday."

Then have them run it with `--days 30`. Something always surfaces.

---

## Where they go next

> "You've got one job in there. The next ones are the database that's been sitting cold,
> your follow-up, your content. Those live in the community — same folder, same brain,
> more jobs.
>
> And if you want me to build the whole thing with you, that's a different conversation.
> Grab a time and let's look at your business."

→ The fit call. **This is the only ask.** Never pitch the cohort from here.
